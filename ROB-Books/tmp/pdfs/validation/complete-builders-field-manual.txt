    NON-NEGOTIABLE SAFETY BOUNDARY

    ROB combines large batteries, high-current brushless motors, chains, treads, flippers, linear motion, long arms,
    machine-tool fabrication, networking, cameras, and software-controlled movement. Qualified adults must
    own the hazard analysis, power protection, emergency-stop architecture, workholding, guarding, lifting, test
    perimeter, and public operation. Never infer a safe circuit or fabrication dimension from a photograph. Never
    treat an ordinary IR sensor, software timeout, or cloud model as safety-rated protective equipment.


   ONE CURRENT ARDUINO, THREE HISTORICAL SKETCHES

   The ROBArduino archive preserves Base, Torso, and Head sketches from an earlier three-Arduino design. The
   builder reports that present-day ROB uses only the Base sketch. This book therefore treats Base behavior
   as the current low-level firmware reference and labels Torso and Head behavior as retired historical evidence.
   Source files cannot prove which binary is flashed today; record the board identity, firmware hash, build envi-
   ronment, and upload date before operation.




Source snapshot and publication cautions
The software was inspected on 2 August 2026. Cerebro contains uncommitted Gemini and autonomy changes, so
publication should record a tag or commit after the builder selects a release. Original gallery photographs were
converted with orientation applied and metadata stripped. Attendee photographs, especially images containing
children, require release review or careful exclusion.
The archived B-COMMAND document prohibits reproduction without permission, and the HengDrive outline is
marked proprietary and confidential. This manual does not reproduce or paraphrase their specifications. It men-

Design principles that recur
Across the sources, several durable principles emerge:

• use prototypes to discover packaging and load problems;
• separate high-level intent from low-level actuator values;
• keep independent stop layers close to each source of failure;
• isolate optional cameras, Python helpers, video, speech, and AI from core motion control;
• preserve service access and label wiring;
• make unsupported capability fail honestly;
• collect evidence before calling a proposal implemented.

Physical and information paths
The robot contains two overlapping architectures:

1. The energy architecture moves stored electrical energy through protection, conversion, controllers, conductors,
   and motors.
2. The information architecture moves intent and measurements through interfaces, authentication, state ma-
   chines, protocols, adapters, firmware, and feedback.

The two paths meet at motor controllers and actuators. Keeping their documentation separate prevents a common
error: assuming that a correct data value proves the high-current circuit is safe, or that a correctly wired motor proves
the command path is authorized.

Manual control, deterministic autonomy, and AI-assisted behavior can all use the same physical plant, but they
should enter through explicit authority gates. EXPERIMENTAL / OPTIONAL Current social-roam autonomy consumes fresh
filtered lidar scans and, near 82 percent of its configured activation radius, attempts to turn and drive toward its
origin. That behavior is not a physical geofence and does not prove containment. Optional Gemini tools declare
high-level action proposals, but most do not yet have executors. Neither path should bypass local validation, the
control-master state, the Arduino deadline, or the physical emergency stop.

   ADVANCED CHANNEL

   Document safety as a control hierarchy, not a list of features. For every hazard, name the prevention, detection,
   mitigation, independent stop, diagnostic coverage, proof test, and residual risk. Ordinary cameras, IR sensors,
   and software timers may support risk reduction but are not automatically safety-rated.

Build the mobile base
The base is the foundation for every later system. Presentation pages 3-9 show left and right wooden side templates,
motor openings, wheel mounts, a battery opening, and the desire to reproduce the form in black powder-coated alu-
minum. Pages 10-20 document the tread tensioner, wheels, sprockets, and shaft collars. Later gallery photographs
show extensive metal and wiring revisions.



Start from interfaces
Before drawing the final plate, freeze or explicitly version these interfaces:

• tread-frame mounting points and full motion envelope;
• drive and idler shaft positions, diameters, retention, and bearing supports;
• motor face, shaft, and cable clearances;
• chain line, sprocket sizes, guard volume, and service access;
• flipper shaft, torque path, rear/front location, and end stops;
• battery envelope, restraint, protection access, and removal path;
• actuator mounts and full extension/retraction envelope;
• lifting, tie-down, stand, cover, and inspection points.

Tread tension and support
The presentation describes aluminum bars with threaded holes that pull the tread assembly outward, free-spinning

The 42 ASCII bytes represent left brake, left signed speed, right brake, right signed speed, flipper brake, flipper

Actuator compact serial
The code sends 0x83 to exit safe start, 0x85 for forward, and 0x86 for reverse. It encodes magnitude in a five-bit low
chunk and remaining high bits. Special input values +3201 and -3201 also clear safe start before becoming +3200
or -3200. Reset should be separated from speed in a future typed protocol.

Base heartbeat ownership
Cerebro evaluates controller snapshots on a timer running at about 10 Hz and treats a snapshot as stale once its
local receipt age exceeds 0.6 seconds. On the first transition from fresh to stale, it writes one neutral/braked serial
frame, stops USB refresh, releases control authority, and allows the Arduino heartbeat to expire. This is a time-based
gate, not a fixed count of three missed messages. The layered behavior is stronger than either deadline alone, but
the Arduino’s own elapsed time and physical stop result still require measurement.



External helpers and dependencies
The OAK-D path uses a supervised Python helper rather than loading DepthAI directly into the control process.
Cerebro can select or create a Python environment and validate the required import. Optional Amber scripts and
tools such as sshpass and ticcmd are preflighted; missing dependencies disable their feature instead of terminating
the app.

Present ROB safely in public
Roles
Assign at least these responsibilities:

• Lead operator: owns arm/disarm and the primary controller.
• Safety spotter: watches the robot and crowd, keeps a hand near the physical E-stop, and may stop without
  asking.
• Educator: explains ROB without distracting the operator.
• Technical support: monitors power, temperature, logs, and recovery.
• Event liaison: coordinates space, charging, barriers, accessibility, and emergency requirements.

One person may fill multiple roles only if workload and risk allow it. Never make the operator answer a long technical
question while ROB moves near visitors.



Pre-show checklist
   current approved software/firmware versions recorded;
   batteries inspected, charged under the approved procedure, and restrained;
   fuses, disconnect, and E-stop visually inspected and function-tested;
   treads, chain, sprockets, guards, shafts, collars, mounts, fasteners, and torque marks inspected;
   arm, neck, flipper, and actuator limits checked in a protected area;
   controllers uniquely paired; unused credentials revoked;
   control and optional video links tested under venue network conditions;
   speed cap and operating mode verified on every controller;
   barrier, route, slope, floor transitions, cables, exits, and charging location inspected;
   camera, microphone, recording, location, and cloud-AI policy displayed;
   fire response, first aid, lift/transport, and fault-recovery plans briefed;
   spare tools and parts controlled so visitors cannot reach them;
   dry rehearsal completed with the full team.



Show-state model
Use explicit visible states: Transport Safe, Display Only, Demo Armed, Moving, Stopped, Faulted, Charging, and
Maintenance. Each transition names who authorizes it and what checklist applies. Reconnecting a controller or
restarting software never silently arms motion.

Teach the architecture without
hiding the hazards
The youth sequence and public website should use the same conceptual spine as the engineering record: system
boundary; energy versus information; sense–estimate–plan–validate–act–measure; mechanics and operating enve-
lope; message grammar and freshness; identity and authority; progressive evidence. The explanation may become
simpler, but it must not become false.



A spiral curriculum for ROB
Return to the same ideas with increasing precision. Ages 8–12 classify inputs, outputs, energy, structure, and feed-
back using paper models. Ages 10–14 graph signals, parse bounded messages, and implement current-limited
LED watchdogs. Ages 10–15 model forces, ratios, tolerances, and test ladders. Ages 12–16 reason about state ma-
chines, networking, trust, leases, perception limits, and bounded autonomy. Adult mentors connect each model to
dated evidence and unresolved hazards.

Facilitation pattern
Begin with a phenomenon, not a vocabulary list. Ask learners to notice; collect competing models without ridicule;

  low-energy kits.

Connection and enrollment matrix

Wi-Fi loss, and server restart. Capture both endpoint states and verify that no application callback occurs before
authentication completes.

  2026-08-10 TRANSPORT LESSON

  During live commissioning, iOS discovered the sole _robctl._udp service but did not surface its Bonjour TXT
  record. The controller now permits a connection attempt only for that sole metadata-less candidate; the ex-
  act certificate pin and reciprocal proof still fail closed for another robot. A second issue exposed QUIC anti-
  amplification behavior: Cerebro attempted to send the challenge before the client address had enough vali-
  dation traffic. The protocol now begins with a padded client pairing-hello carrying a registry-checked device
  identifier, then sends the fresh challenge. Treat this as a dated implementation note pending tagged release
  and regression fixtures.




Measure the whole stop chain

speed, and complete physical stop. Inject joystick release, app suspension, cable removal, malformed frames,
serial noise, stale sensors, helper failure, and E-stop activation separately. Report both software state and measured
motion. A green authenticated-link indicator proves identity and readiness; it does not prove command freshness,
brake behavior, or stopping distance.
